# Werkbord

Werkbord is a local-first team workspace for AI agents. People plan and approve the work on one board, agents do it in their own Git worktrees, and nothing merges without a person. It organizes tasks, agent runs, schedules, and reviews around local Git repositories. It connects to Claude Code, Codex, Gemini CLI and OpenCode through the person’s own command-line tools. Phone access lets users follow the same work remotely while execution remains on their computer.

Werkbord Team coordinates people, ticket ownership, reviews, and reported repository state. Each teammate keeps their own local runner and credentials. Product managers and designers write tickets, follow progress and sign off from a browser; simpler flows for people who do not code are planned. The public site offers early access through a waitlist. Documentation also serves people who already have Team access and need installation instructions.

The website should quickly explain the essential Personal and Team workflows. Interactive product examples carry the detail. Keep marketing copy concise, concrete, and free of em dashes. Technical figures must come from product evidence and state any qualification, such as performance measured in tests.

Positioning: the site speaks of a team workspace for AI agents that bridges people who code and people who do not. State only what ships today. Planned work (simpler flows for non-developers) may appear once, labelled as planned, in llms.txt, never in a headline, title or description. `node --test scripts/test-seo.cjs` enforces that.

Preserve the established pixel mark, Geist typography, neutral surfaces, cobalt and amber accents, and light/dark themes. Use restrained motion and respect reduced-motion preferences. The public footer contains the copyright line; the launch page offers the waitlist and the demo.

The demo’s in-app help is task-oriented and based on the product repository’s guides. The public launch page has no separate documentation pages; do not link to removed pages.

One product, one build, two plans: Free includes Personal and teams of two; teams of three or more need Premium for every member. Premium is a per-person offline key, a one-time purchase not yet on sale. No per-team licence or separate Premium download. The demo is scripted and does not establish production readiness.
